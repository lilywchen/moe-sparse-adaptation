\section{Limitations and broader relevance}

One cellular-microscopy benchmark does not establish a universal law of conditional adaptation.
RxRx1 is unusually useful mechanistically because the same perturbations repeat across many
experiments, but conclusions may not transfer to histopathology, radiology, or other acquisition
processes. Environment metadata is available for analysis and balancing, which may not hold in
every deployment.

The substrate gate can only qualify the released checkpoints and bounded interfaces tested here.
The fixed six-to-five Cell-DINO map is biologically motivated, but merging separately acquired
actin and Golgi channels cannot exactly reproduce a simultaneous Cell Painting acquisition.
Channel-Adaptive DINO avoids that mapping but differs in architecture and parameter count, so the
qualification comparison is not a general backbone ranking. The eventual MoE claim is identified
only within the selected substrate against its exact matched dense control.

The broader value is an auditable decision about whether conditional capacity is appropriate for
heterogeneous scientific adaptation. A positive result must survive parameter matching,
replication, held-out-experiment folds, and route counterfactuals. A negative result is useful only
after the dense substrate learns the task and the hypothesized gradient conflict is actually
measured; otherwise it is merely an instrument failure.
