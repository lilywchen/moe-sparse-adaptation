\section{Scientific hypotheses}

\paragraph{H1: dense adaptation exhibits cross-batch gradient interference.}
For the same perturbation objective, gradients contributed by different training experiments are
less aligned at the converted FFN than gradients sampled within an experiment. A dense FFN must
average these conflicting updates; top-1 experts can give compatible examples partially separate
update paths. We measure experiment-stratified gradient cosine, norm, and conflict rate before and
after routing. The hypothesis is falsified if gradients are already aligned or MoE does not reduce
within-path conflict.

\paragraph{H2: useful routes represent reusable structure rather than experiment identity.}
Routes may be batch-decodable because acquisition is visible, but they should not collapse into one
expert per training experiment. Useful assignments should recur across experiments, remain stable
under geometric augmentation, and retain balanced usage on held-out experiments. Frozen and
post-training randomized routers test whether structured routes are actually required.

\paragraph{H3: conditional gain is robustness, not parameter count.}
The primary estimand is the paired OOD-validation difference between MoE and an exact
total-parameter-matched dense-wide FFN. A credible gain also improves worst-experiment accuracy and
the ID--OOD gap while preserving ID accuracy. A gain only over the smaller original encoder is a
capacity result, not evidence for conditional computation.
