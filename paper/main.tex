\begin{abstract}
Scientific images are collected across experiments, instruments, and clinical sites whose
acquisition signatures can change without changing the underlying scientific target. Sparse
mixture-of-experts (MoE) models offer conditional capacity that could absorb heterogeneous
adaptation demands, but the same mechanism could instead specialize to acquisition nuisance and
fail on unseen environments. We ask when sparse conditional capacity improves supervised
adaptation of a pretrained vision encoder under acquisition shift. After a predeclared dense-
substrate competence gate on RxRx1 cellular microscopy, we upcycle one middle feed-forward block
of a microscopy-pretrained ViT and compare it with an exactly initialized,
total-parameter-matched dense block. The primary outcome is accuracy on unseen experiments; seen-
experiment accuracy and the ID--OOD gap distinguish failed task learning from genuine batch
transfer. Gradient-conflict, routing, and counterfactual probes test whether any matched gain comes
from separating interfering acquisition environments rather than added capacity or batch
memorization. A negative matched result remains informative because the substrate, parameter
budget, and failure regime are validated before sparse capacity is tested.
\end{abstract}
